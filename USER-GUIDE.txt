===============================================================
 CBM ARTICLE CONVERTER - USER GUIDE
 Version 1.5.0
===============================================================

This turns a finished article into HTML that can be pasted
into a Squarespace Code Block, with working footnotes, pull
quotes and Scripture formatting.

It takes articles from two places:

  a Word document   (.docx)
  an InDesign export (.html)

There are two audiences for this guide. Editors who run the
app want the first half. The person building the web page
wants the section headed FOR WHOEVER BUILDS THE PAGE.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 GETTING STARTED
---------------------------------------------------------------

The app is a single file. Nothing needs installing. Copy it
where you like and double-click it.

The first time it runs on a machine, the system will object,
because the app is not signed:

  Windows   "Windows protected your PC". Click More info,
            then Run anyway.

  macOS     It simply refuses. Right-click (or Control-click)
            the app, choose Open, then confirm. After that it
            opens normally.

This happens once per machine, not once per article.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 THE WINDOW
---------------------------------------------------------------

Three buttons:

  Choose article...     pick one or more files to convert
  Convert               convert them
  Add house styles...   put the house styles into an author's
                        Word file, see further down

Below those, a line shows which house template the app is
using, with a Change button beside it. The template is
remembered between sessions.

The large panel reports what happened: how many footnotes were
linked, where pull quotes went, the word count, and any
warnings. Read it. Nearly every problem announces itself there
before it reaches the website.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 BEFORE YOU CONVERT: WORD ARTICLES
---------------------------------------------------------------

The converter reads Word styles. It cannot read formatting
applied by hand: a paragraph indented with the ruler is not a
block quotation as far as any software is concerned, it is
just an indented paragraph.

Use these styles:

  Heading 2       top-level sections
  Heading 3       subsections
  Heading 4       third level
  Block Text      block quotations
  Blurb           the summary that opens the article
  Author Bio      the contributor's biography
  Article Note    "second in a series", and the like
  Side Bar        a pull quote
  Verse Block     poetry or Scripture set as lines
  Body Text       ordinary paragraphs

Italics typed directly are kept. So are Word's own footnotes.
Footnotes typed by hand as plain numbers are not footnotes and
cannot be made into them afterwards.

Title and Author are found by their labels in the text:

  Title: The Faulty Hermeneutics of the Assumption
  Author: Joshua Daugereau

Leave those labels in. They are stripped during conversion.

For Verse Block, use ONE paragraph per stanza and press
Shift+Enter between the lines inside it. Do not make each line
its own paragraph.

For Side Bar, copy the sentence you want to feature, paste it
as its own paragraph, and apply the style. Edit the copy so it
reads on its own: trim the middle with an ellipsis, put any
added word in square brackets. Both survive the matching.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 BEFORE YOU CONVERT: INDESIGN ARTICLES
---------------------------------------------------------------

Export with File > Export and choose HTML, listed as
"HTML (Classic)" in recent versions. Do NOT use "HTML5
Publication" or "Publish Online": those make a page-flipping
viewer, not an article.

Leave "Generate CSS" switched on, and KEEP THE
"-web-resources" FOLDER BESIDE THE .html FILE. The converter
reads InDesign's stylesheet to work out which of its styles
mean italic, bold and superscript, because InDesign names them
things like "CharOverride-1". Without that folder the italics
are lost, and the app will warn you.

Pull quotes need no special placement. Put them in a text
frame, apply the Side Bar paragraph style, and let the frame
sit wherever suits the layout. InDesign pushes unanchored
frames out of the article on export, and the converter puts
each quote back beside the paragraph it quotes.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 CONVERTING
---------------------------------------------------------------

Choose one or more articles, then press Convert.

Two files appear beside the original, named with the date and
time of the run. THEY ARE NOT THE SAME.

  20261004_1032_Gurtaev Water Baptism.html
  20261004_1032_Gurtaev Water Baptism.txt

  Open the .html in a browser to CHECK the article.
  Open the .txt in Notepad to COPY the code.

The .html carries the styling with it, so it looks right on its
own when you check it. The .txt does not: on the site the
styling lives in Squarespace's Custom CSS, where one edit
reaches every article ever published.

ALWAYS COPY FROM THE .txt. Pasting from the .html would put a
copy of the stylesheet into that one article, and it would then
ignore any later change made site-wide.

Check the .html first. Click a raised footnote number: it
should jump to the note. Click the note's number: it should
jump back. On a long article try the first note, a two-digit
one, and the last.

The .html will look plain, with no fonts or colours. That is
correct. Squarespace supplies all of that.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 WHAT THE CONVERTER DOES
---------------------------------------------------------------

At the top of the finished file, above the code, in plain text:

  TITLE
  The Faulty Hermeneutics of the Assumption

  AUTHOR
  Joshua Daugereau

  ----- paste everything below this line into the Code Block -----

Those two are cut out and pasted into Squarespace's own boxes.
Being above that line, they cannot be pasted in by accident.

Then the article itself:

  the blurb, set larger than the body as an opening
  any note about the article, in italic beneath it
  the reading time, as "27 MIN READ" with a clock
  a hairline rule closing the front matter

  the body, with headings 2 and 3 centred and heading 4
  left-aligned and italic

  pull quotes, alternating left and right down the article,
  each carrying links to share that sentence

  a hairline rule
  the author bio, centred and italic
  the footnotes

Footnote numbers at the bottom link back up to the callout.
Links to other websites open in a new tab. Footnote links do
not: they jump within the same page.

A paragraph that is mostly Hebrew is set right to left and
right-aligned. Greek is tagged as Greek. A Hebrew word inside
an English sentence is left alone; browsers handle that.

The reading time counts the article body at 200 words a
minute. Footnotes, pull quotes and front matter are left out.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 IF A PULL QUOTE DOES NOT MATCH
---------------------------------------------------------------

A pull quote has to repeat a sentence from the article so it
can be matched to its paragraph. If one has been reworded, the
app shows you the text and asks:

  Yes   leave that quote out and carry on
  No    abandon this file and write nothing

Choosing No lets you correct the wording and convert again.

The usual cause is someone tidying the sentence while copying
it. Mark additions with [square brackets] and cuts with an
ellipsis and it will still match.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 ADDING HOUSE STYLES TO AN AUTHOR'S FILE
---------------------------------------------------------------

Authors send documents with no styles, or with styles they
invented. Rather than rebuild the file and risk losing things,
this copies the house styles INTO it.

Press "Add house styles...", choose the author's .docx, and
confirm which template to use. A new file appears beside the
original:

  <original name> (house styles).docx

Open that and the house styles are in the Styles pane, ready
to apply. Nothing else changed: the text, comments, tables and
tracked changes are exactly as the author sent them. Styles
the document already has are left alone.


===============================================================
 FOR WHOEVER BUILDS THE PAGE
===============================================================

You will be given two files per article. Use the .txt.

1. Open the .txt. It opens in Notepad.

2. At the top, above the marked line, are the TITLE and the
   AUTHOR as plain text. Cut those and paste them into
   Squarespace's own title and author boxes.

3. Select everything BELOW the marked line and copy it.

4. In Squarespace, add a CODE BLOCK. Not a Text Block: a Text
   Block strips out the markers the footnotes rely on, and
   every footnote stops working.

5. Delete the placeholder content, paste, and make sure the
   block type is HTML with "Display Source" unticked.

6. Save and look at the PUBLISHED page. Code Blocks often do
   not render inside the editor, so the editor preview proves
   nothing.

7. Click a few footnotes on the live page.

Images are not carried across. If the article has photographs,
place them as ordinary Image Blocks and split the text into
two Code Blocks around each one. Footnote links still work
across blocks, because it is all one page.

ONE THING TO SET UP ONCE, SITE-WIDE
-----------------------------------

Pull quotes need nothing. The article carries its own styling,
including the floating into the margins on wide screens, so
there is nothing to add for those.

What does need setting up once is Hebrew. The site font does
not position Hebrew vowel points correctly. Upload a Hebrew
font through Manage Custom Files in Design > Custom CSS, then
add a second face under the SAME family name as the site font,
scoped to the Hebrew range:

  @font-face {
    font-family: 'Tinos';
    src: url();
    unicode-range: U+0590-05FF, U+FB1D-FB4F, U+200F;
  }

Place the cursor between the brackets of url() and click the
uploaded font in Manage Custom Files to insert its address.
This block must come after whatever declares the site font.
Latin text is unaffected, and the Hebrew font is downloaded
only on pages that actually contain Hebrew.

---------------------------------------------------------------
 WHEN SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG
---------------------------------------------------------------

Read the report panel first. It says what it did.

"no stylesheet found; italics may be lost"
  An InDesign export without its "-web-resources" folder
  beside it. Put them back together and convert again.

Block quotations came out as ordinary paragraphs
  They were indented by hand rather than given the Block Text
  style. Apply the style and convert again.

A heading is not a heading
  It used a style that only looks like a heading. Use Word's
  own Heading 2, 3 and 4.

Footnotes do nothing on the published page
  A Text Block was used instead of a Code Block.

Share links on pull quotes do nothing
  The small script at the end of the file was not pasted in.
  Copy the whole thing, including everything after the last
  paragraph.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 WHICH BUILD MADE A FILE
---------------------------------------------------------------

Every finished file begins with a hidden line:

  <!-- CBM Article Tools, Version 1.5.0 - converted 2026-10-04
       10:32 - from: Gurtaev_Water_Baptism.html -->

It never shows on the page. It survives into the Code Block,
so a published article can always be traced to the build that
made it. The build number is also in the app's title bar.

---------------------------------------------------------------
 DROPPING AN ARTICLE ONTO THE APP
---------------------------------------------------------------

Instead of opening the app and choosing a file, drag the
article onto the application itself. A shortcut works just as
well, so one on the desktop is worth making.

Several files can be dropped at once.

The app opens and asks first, naming the file and listing the
settings it is about to use:

  Convert now
  Open, don't convert yet
  Cancel

That middle option loads the article and waits, so Preferences
can be checked or changed before anything is written. Cancel
clears it and does nothing.

The confirmation exists because settings may have moved since
last time. Rather than finding out afterwards, the ones that
are not at their defaults are listed before anything happens.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 THE DESKTOP SHORTCUT
---------------------------------------------------------------

Each version is a separate file, so without tidying up the
Desktop collects a shortcut per version.

If you already have a shortcut to an older version, the app
offers to repoint it after a download and remove the old one.
If you have none, it does not ask: people who do not use
shortcuts are never interrupted about them.

On a Mac the download is a zip, so expand it first and then use
Preferences > Desktop shortcut to point at the app.

Preferences > Desktop shortcut can be used at any time, whether
or not a shortcut is there already, to point at whichever copy
of the app you want, wherever it is kept.

Only shortcuts named after this app are removed. Nothing else
on the Desktop is touched.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 PHOTOGRAPHS
---------------------------------------------------------------

Put the picture in the Word document where it belongs, sized
and wrapped the way it should appear. The converter reads both:

  how wide it is shown   becomes a share of the text column,
                         so the photograph keeps its own shape

  how text wraps round   in line, or wrapped left or right

To wrap text round a picture in Word, select it, then Layout
Options, then Square, and drag it to the side you want.

ALT TEXT

  Right-click the picture, then Alt Text, and describe it in a
  sentence. This is read aloud to anyone using a screen reader
  and is used by search engines. The converter reports any
  picture without it, but converts anyway.

CAPTIONS

  A paragraph in Word's own Caption style, directly above or
  below the picture, travels with it. Nothing is reported if
  there is no caption.

WHAT COMES OUT

  The pictures are saved to a folder beside the article, named
  after it. The article itself carries a marked block where
  each one belongs, because a picture has no web address until
  it has been uploaded.

  The .html shows the real pictures from that folder, so the
  article looks right when you check it.

PUTTING THEM IN

  Whoever builds the page uploads the pictures to the site,
  then uses Designer > Place the pictures, pastes each address,
  and gets a copy of the article with the pictures in place.

  Leaving an address blank keeps that marker for later.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 THE BYLINE AND AUTHOR PROFILES
---------------------------------------------------------------

Apply the Author style to the line naming the authors. Several
names can be separated by commas, "and", or an ampersand; the
tool keeps the order you wrote them in and punctuates the
byline itself.

Each name becomes a link to that author's profile page, built
from the name:

  Scott Callaham   ->   /profile/scott-callaham

The address is worked out from the name every time, so the
article and the page always agree. Where a profile page does
not exist yet, the tool says so above the code, giving the
exact address to create:

  PROFILE PAGES NEEDED
    Kyle Dunham
      /profile/kyle-dunham

Make the page at exactly that address or the link will be dead.

The AUTHOR line above the code is still there, because the
post's own author field should still be set in Squarespace even
though the byline no longer comes from it.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 THE DESIGNER MENU
---------------------------------------------------------------

For whoever builds and maintains the web pages.

ARTICLE STYLES

  Every style an article uses, laid out by element: the blurb,
  byline, article note, reading time, bio, headings, verse,
  pull quotes, share icons, rules and footnotes.

  "Preview in browser" writes a small sample article with the
  current settings and opens it, so nothing has to be imagined.

COPY THE ARTICLE STYLESHEET

  Produces the stylesheet to paste into Squarespace's Custom
  CSS. Paste it once; from then on a change in Article Styles
  and a fresh paste restyles EVERY article on the site,
  including ones published long ago.

  The block is marked BEGIN and END. Anything written outside
  those lines is yours and survives a fresh paste.

BUILD AN ARTICLE CARD

  Paste an article's address and it reads the title, date,
  excerpt and picture from the site, then builds the HTML for
  a link card to put on an author's profile page. Every field
  can be edited first, and a blank one is left out.

  Turn the styling off when adding a card to a list that
  already has it.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 PREFERENCES
---------------------------------------------------------------

Preferences > Preferences... controls what an article contains
and how it is written.

WHAT GOES IN, AND IN WHAT ORDER

  Blurb, Article note, Reading time, Share this article and
  Author bio can each be included or left out, and moved.

  Select one and use Move up or Move down. "Include / omit"
  ticks and unticks it.

  The article body is always included, but it can be moved, so
  the bio can sit above it if that is what the site wants.
  Footnotes always come last, because they are built from the
  body and nothing sensible follows them.

  A hairline rule is drawn wherever the body meets something
  else, so you do not have to place those yourself.

HEADINGS

  Left, centred, right, or left to the site. The last leaves
  headings alone so the site's own settings decide.

READING TIME

  Words a minute. 200 is the default; lower it for a heavier
  read, raise it for a lighter one.

STYLING

  The article normally carries its own styling, which is what
  makes pull quotes float without anything set up site-wide.
  Turning it off leaves everything to the site, and heading
  alignment goes with it.

WHERE FINISHED FILES GO

  Beside the original, or a folder of your choosing.

RESETTING

  "Reset to defaults" in the dialog, or Preferences > Reset
  everything to defaults, puts it all back. The house template
  is remembered separately and is not affected.

Any setting that is not at its default is noted in the hidden
comment at the top of each converted file, so an article that
looks unexpected can always be explained.


---------------------------------------------------------------
 CHECKING FOR A NEWER VERSION
---------------------------------------------------------------

The app checks by itself a moment after it opens, so nobody
has to remember. If something newer exists it says so; if not,
it notes that quietly in the report and carries on.

There is a tick-box beside the template line to turn that off,
and a "Check for updates" button to ask at any time.

If there is, it shows what changed and offers to download it.
You choose where it goes. The new version is saved ALONGSIDE
this one rather than replacing it, so you can go back if the
new one misbehaves.

Each version is named for itself, as in

    CBM Article Converter 1.0.0.exe

so the two never get confused.

If the check cannot reach GitHub it notes it in the report
and tells you where to download by hand. Nothing breaks either way; the
converter does not need the internet to do its work.
